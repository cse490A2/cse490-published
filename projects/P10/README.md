# Project 10: Vibe Coding Final Project

So far in this class we’ve focused on *coding* agents.  For this project you’ll develop an agent that can do something else – of your choosing (e.g., create games, help you cook, fitness coach, etc.).  First you’ll write a short proposal for an idea.  Then you’ll build it and measure how well it works.

We’re significantly increasing your token budget (\$100 each\!) for this project, and you’ll have access to top models (e.g., Sol, Opus, DeepSeek).  These expensive models burn through tokens quickly though, so make sure you don’t run out\!  You can check your budget as follows:  open the Wizard, click on “key status” next to attendance to see your LiteLLM models and spend budget. You’re welcome to use liteLLM, purple, and your own subscriptions.

**Due Dates:**

* **Dec 1: Project Proposal**  
  * *feedback from course staff by Dec 3rd (start building date)*  
* **Dec 15:  Everything else**

Top projects (chosen by staff \+ AI) will be announced and featured on the web page\!

**Part 1: Project Proposal**

In a paragraph or two, describe your application and its intended impact.

*Criteria*

- Generative AI must be part of the core design, but you're not limited to text.  
- Your application must have a **specialized interface** (UI that’s not just a text entry box)   
- You may reuse tools from your harness, but you should add new functionality.  
- Your design should incorporate at least one concept introduced in class, such as tool calling, skills, hooks, MCP, system prompts, multi-agent orchestration, or decision models.  
- it shouldn’t be \*just\* a chat-bot-style code harness (e.g., Claude Code-style)

You may build using your own course harness or any other harness (Claude, Codex, Lovable, etc.)

Submit your proposal on Canvas, and the course staff will provide a review with feedback.

**Part 2: The Build**

Build your application with AI, the same way you built your projects in class. Once it works, use it for its purpose and measure the results.

*Criteria*

- Your application must run. Someone else should be able to start it from your README.  
- Your build should match your proposal.  
- Your measurement must be a number with a comparison (e.g. the same task done by hand, or with a general chat interface).  
- You must have a minimum of 30 turns with your agent when building your design (you’ll need to record and submit your prompt history)

To change your idea after the review, check with the course staff first.

*Turn-In*  
On Canvas, 

* submit a zip of your project (without your keys)  
* File containing your prompt history  
* and a voice over screen recording (under three minutes) of your application in use.  
* 1-2 page report containing  
  * why you built it  
  * what you learned  
  * surprises (worked well, not so well)

Below are some examples:

* [Muse from Meta](https://muse.ai). A personal agent that books, shops and answers email, and asks for your approval before it acts.  
* [Playground from Google Labs](https://labs.google/playground). Platform for generating games based on a prompt.  
* [Underlord from Descript](https://descript.com/underlord). A video co-editor that turns a raw recording into an edited video from a text instruction.  
* [AI Village from AI Digest](https://theaidigest.org/village). A public community of agents from different labs who share a group chat and work toward a common goal while anyone watches.  
* [Suno](https://suno.com): A text-to-song generator
